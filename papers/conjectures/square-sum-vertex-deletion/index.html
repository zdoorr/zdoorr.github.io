A One-Vertex-Deletion Conjecture for Square-Sum Hamiltonian Cycles

Justin Du·14 November 2026

Abstract

Let Gₙ join distinct integers in [n] when their sum is a square. We conjecture that Gₙ − d has a Hamiltonian cycle for every n ≥ 71 and every vertex d, strengthening known ordinary square-sum cycles. We prove that 71 is the first possible threshold, establish minimum-degree and edge-count asymptotics, and give an exact one-rotation closure criterion. A specified 71-cycle yields a local counterexample to one-rotation deletion repair, while longer rotations succeed. Independently checked witnesses cover all 4775 deletion instances with 71 ≤ n ≤ 120. These results separate arithmetic sparsity, local repair obstructions, and finite existence evidence from the still-open infinite assertion.

What is proved, and what remains open

The universal assertion is unproved. Supplied cycles certify every deletion at orders 71 through 120. The one-rotation counterexample concerns a specified starting cycle and repair operation, not the conjecture.

Status & AI use

This is a proposed research problem with partial results, not a general proof or an externally peer-reviewed publication. Generative AI contributed substantially to exploration, derivations, manuscript text and verification code. The proofs received internal review and bounded exact checks; no independent human peer review or proof-assistant formalization is claimed.
