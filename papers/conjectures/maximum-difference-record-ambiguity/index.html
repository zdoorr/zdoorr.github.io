Maximum Ambiguity of Ordered Adjacent-Difference Records

Justin Du·14 November 2026

Abstract

An ordered adjacent-difference record retains the absolute differences of consecutive entries of a permutation. Its ambiguity is the number of permutations giving that exact word. We study its maximum M(n) without asserting an unsupported exact formula. We prove a signed-walk characterization, endpoint-sensitive gluing, and existence of the global exponential growth rate, with a lower bound greater than 1.28142. We completely classify the infinite family of two unit-step blocks separated by one jump, and derive constant-run and fixed-endpoint antichain bounds. Examples refute simple structural heuristics, including the necessity of a unit step in every optimizer. The underlying reconstruction mapping has prior literature; maximum fiber structure remains the central open problem.

What is proved, and what remains open

This note poses an open extremal problem rather than an unsupported general formula. The difference mapping has prior literature. Global values at orders 9 through 16 are explicitly attributed to archived searches.

Status & AI use

This is a proposed research problem with partial results, not a general proof or an externally peer-reviewed publication. Generative AI contributed substantially to exploration, derivations, manuscript text and verification code. The proofs received internal review and bounded exact checks; no independent human peer review or proof-assistant formalization is claimed.
